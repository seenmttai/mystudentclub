Cold emailing HRs for industrial training: templates that get replies

Published on 30 September 2026

Industrial Training 6 min read

Many companies that take industrial trainees never post the role. They hire from CVs that land in the right inbox at the right time. That makes a well-written cold mail one of the best tools you have in the months before your industrial training (IT) eligibility date.

This guide covers who to write to, what to write, and how to follow up without becoming a nuisance. It includes six templates you can adapt.

Before you write a single mail

Have three things ready:

  • Your IT eligibility date, the day you complete 12 months of articleship. It goes in every subject line, because it's the first thing HR needs to know.
  • Two target domains, such as internal audit and FP&A. Don't write "any finance role".
  • A one-page CV saved as a PDF, named something like FirstName-LastName-CA-IT-CV.pdf. The CV guide shows how to write one.

Timing. Start about three months before your eligibility date, the same month you tell your principal. That's when HR teams can still plan a seat for you. The 4-month roadmap has the full timeline.

Who to write to

In order of preference:

  1. A senior in the team you want to join. That could be the financial controller, the head of internal audit or the tax head. They feel the need for help most directly.
  2. The HR or talent acquisition person who handles finance hiring.
  3. A general careers address, only when you can't find a person.

Where to find them:

  • the company's careers page and annual report (the report names the finance leadership);
  • LinkedIn: search "<company> finance" or "<company> talent acquisition" and filter by your city;
  • seniors from your firm who did IT at that company;
  • the free Industrial Training Hiring Companies List in our IT resources.

Keep ICAI's rules in mind. A company needs ICAI empanelment and a minimum size to take an industrial trainee: ₹10 crore of fixed assets, or ₹50 crore of turnover, or ₹1 crore of paid-up capital with a ₹5 crore net worth. It also needs at least two full-time CAs of three years' standing. So spend your time on companies likely to qualify: listed companies, large private groups and multinationals, not small local businesses.

Six rules for a mail that gets read

  1. One company per mail, sent from your own email ID. Never CC or BCC a list of companies. It shows, and it kills your chances.
  2. A subject line that says everything: your stage, your date and your domain.
  3. Under 150 words. HR often reads on a phone.
  4. One line that's specific to the company: a business line, a plant, a recent expansion. It proves the mail isn't a mass mailing.
  5. Your CV attached as a PDF, not as a Drive link.
  6. A weekday morning send. A mail that arrives on a Tuesday morning is easier to act on than one sent at midnight on a Saturday.

Template 1: first mail to HR

Subject: Industrial training from <month year>: CA Inter, <domain>

Dear <name>,

I'm a CA student at <firm>, <city>, and I complete 12 months of articleship on <date>. I'm looking for industrial training in <domain> from <month>, for <9 to 12> months.

In articleship I've worked on <two specific things, with numbers: for example, the statutory audit of four manufacturing companies and monthly GST returns for eight clients>. I'd like to train at <company> because <one specific reason>.

My CV is attached. Could you let me know if <company> is taking industrial trainees for this period, or who I should write to?

Regards,
<Name> | <phone> | <city>

Template 2: to a finance head (shorter)

Senior people read less. Get to the point in three lines.

Subject: CA industrial trainee for your <team>, from <month year>

Dear <name>,

I complete my first year of articleship at <firm> on <date> and I'm looking for industrial training in <team, e.g. controllership>. In articleship I've <one specific thing, with a number>.

If your team is taking an industrial trainee this year, I'd be grateful for a chance to interview. My CV is attached.

Regards,
<Name> | <phone>

Template 3: the follow-up, five working days later

Reply on the same thread, so they see your first mail.

Dear <name>,

I'm following up on my note below about industrial training from <month>. I'm still keen on <company> and can come in for an interview any day next week.

Regards,
<Name>

Follow up once. If there's still no reply, move on, and try a different person at the same company a few weeks later.

Template 4: asking a senior for a referral

Send this on WhatsApp or LinkedIn to someone who did IT at the company.

Hi <name>, I'm <name>, an article at <firm> (you were here in <year>). I'm applying for industrial training in <domain> from <month>. Would you be open to passing my CV to your team, or telling me who handles IT hiring at <company>? I can send a two-line summary you can forward. Thank you!

Make it easy for them. Offer something they can forward in one tap.

Template 5: a LinkedIn connection note

Hi <name>, I'm a CA student looking for industrial training in <domain> from <month>. I'd value connecting, and would be grateful to know if <company> takes industrial trainees.

LinkedIn limits the length of connection notes, and this one fits. Once they accept, send Template 1 as a message, with your CV.

Template 6: when they reply

Dear <name>,

Thank you for getting back to me. I'm available on <two or three slots> and will bring <anything they asked for>. If I'm selected, could you also tell me who I'd be working with?

Regards,
<Name>

The last question is useful: it tells you who your trainer is likely to be. Under ICAI's rules, that person must be an ICAI member at the company.

Mistakes that get mails ignored

  • Writing "Dear Sir/Madam" when the person's name is on LinkedIn.
  • A subject line that just says "Application" or "CV".
  • Asking for "any opportunity". Say which role you want.
  • Attaching five documents. Send one PDF.
  • Pasting your whole CV into the body of the mail.
  • Following up every day.

A weekly routine that works

  • Monday: add 15 new companies to your tracker, each with a named contact.
  • Tuesday to Thursday: send five tailored mails a day.
  • Friday: follow up on everything you sent the week before, and update the tracker.

Keep the tracker simple, with one column each for company, contact, role, date sent, follow-up date, reply and next step. It's the difference between 40 random mails and a real search. Apply to posted vacancies alongside your cold mails.
