Industrial training: the 4-month roadmap

Published on 30 September 2026

Industrial Training 7 min read

Industrial training (IT) is the part of your articleship that you spend inside a company: in finance, internal audit, tax or treasury, at a business rather than a CA firm. The students who get the company and the domain they want rarely start on the day they become eligible. They start four months earlier.

This is that plan, month by month, with every ICAI form you'll need.

The rules in plain words

  • Eligibility. You can join IT once you've passed CA Intermediate (both groups) and completed at least 12 months of articleship. Call that date E, your eligibility date.
  • Length. IT lasts 9 to 12 months, in the last year of your articleship. It replaces the rest of your articleship, and you can't go back to the firm afterwards.
  • Your start window. You can start any time from E to about three months after it, because at least nine months of articleship must be left when you start. Excess leave moves these dates.
  • Notice. ICAI asks you to tell your principal at least three months before your IT start date. For a start on day E, that means around month nine of articleship.
  • Stipend. At least ₹15,000 a month.
  • Your Final. IT ends when your articleship ends, so your end date still decides your first CA Final attempt. If your articleship ends between 1 May and 31 October, you can sit the next May Final. If it ends between 1 November and 30 April, you sit the next November Final. Excess leave that pushes you past either date costs a whole six-month cycle.

Which companies can take you

From 1 January 2026, a company needs ICAI empanelment to take industrial trainees. It must meet one of these tests:

  • fixed assets of ₹10 crore or more, or
  • turnover of ₹50 crore or more, or
  • paid-up capital of ₹1 crore and net worth of ₹5 crore.

It must also have at least two full-time Chartered Accountants who have been members for three years or more. Your trainer is an ICAI member at the company. An Associate member can train one industrial trainee and a Fellow can train two.

Before you accept any offer, ask HR two questions. Is the company empanelled with ICAI for industrial training? Who will be my trainer?

Month 1 (four months before E): sort out your articleship

  • Log in to the ICAI Self-Service Portal (SSP) and check your articleship record: your principal, your start date and your Form 103 status.
  • Work out your real E. It's your start date plus 12 months, plus any leave beyond the allowance.
  • Put your documents in one folder:
    • your articleship deed and your Form 103 acknowledgement;
    • your Inter mark sheet and ICITSS certificate;
    • ID proof and a passport photo.
  • Pick two domains. Statutory or internal audit, FP&A, direct or indirect tax, treasury, controllership. Pick two, not five.
  • Rewrite your CV around your first year. Describe your clients by industry and size, then the areas you handled, the tools you used and what you found or fixed. The CV guide has before-and-after examples.
  • List 30 target companies in your city and your domains. The free Industrial Training Hiring Companies List in our IT resources is a good place to start.

Month 2 (three months before E): tell your principal and start applying

  • Have the conversation in person. Tell your principal you plan to move to industrial training around E, and how you'll hand over your work. Then confirm it by email. A principal who hears it from you first, three months ahead, is far more likely to sign Form 109 without friction.
  • Ask for a no-objection letter (NOC) if your target companies want one. Some ask for it before they issue an offer. It isn't an ICAI form, so check what each company needs.
  • Start applying. Use posted vacancies, company careers pages and direct mails to HR. Many companies that take industrial trainees never post the role, which is why cold mails work. The cold-emailing guide has six templates.
  • Join a batch if you want structure. Join the MSC Industrial Training Program this month. You'll finish it with time left to apply.

Month 3 (two months before E): volume, referrals, interviews

  • Send five good applications a day, each with a CV tailored to the role. Apply within two days of a post going up.
  • Ask for referrals. Message five seniors who did IT last year and ask one question: how did you get in?
  • Prepare for interviews. Expect detailed questions on your articleship work and on the basics of your domain: Ind AS for finance roles, internal controls for audit roles, and GST and income tax for tax roles. Many companies also give an Excel test.
  • Keep a tracker: company, role, contact, date applied, stage and next step.

Two months is enough if you start this week. Four to eight weeks from application to offer is normal.

Month 4 (one month before E): offers, checks, paperwork

When an offer arrives, check four things before you say yes:

  • the company is empanelled for IT, and your trainer is named;
  • the offer letter shows your start and end dates, and your training runs 9 to 12 months within the last year of your articleship;
  • the stipend is at least ₹15,000 a month;
  • the domain is the one you wanted.

Then prepare the switch with your principal:

  • Form 109 terminates your articleship. File it on the SSP on the ground "Joining industrial training", signed by you and your principal. After the first year, ICAI takes about 15 working days to process it.
  • Your offer letter goes with it.
  • A consent letter from the company is also needed if you still have excess leave to serve.

Eligibility day and after

  • On or after E: your Form 109 goes in, and you join the company.
  • Within 30 days of starting: file Form 104, the industrial training agreement with your trainer, on the SSP with the deed on stamp paper. The same 30-day rule and late fees apply as for your articleship registration.
  • When you finish: file Form 105 within 30 days.

No offer by E? If you've already given notice, you still have up to about three months to start. Keep applying every day, and use the time for mock interviews.

If your principal won't sign

There's a route that doesn't need your principal's signature: Form 120, open to you after six months of articleship. It's slow, because ICAI first sends your principal three monthly reminders. In practice, the conversation in Month 2 matters more than any form. Start it early and keep it friendly.
