Semi-qualified CA jobs: roles, where to find them, and study leave

Published on 30 September 2026

Semi-Qualified CA 5 min read

You've finished articleship, and CA Final is still ahead. That makes you what recruiters call a semi-qualified CA. It's a stronger position than it may feel: you have two or three years of real work behind you, and companies and firms hire for exactly that.

This guide covers whether to work, which roles fit, where to find them, how to handle salary and study leave, and how to plan for both results.

Why so many students work at this stage

Under the new scheme, you can sit CA Final only if your articleship was complete at least six months before the first day of the exam month. Final is held in May and November. So almost every new-scheme student has a gap of six months or more between articleship and Final. Many use it to earn, and to build experience in the domain they want.

Others are semi-qualified for longer: they have one Final group left, or they're between attempts. This guide is for all of you.

Should you work at all?

Work makes sense if:

  • you have one group left, or you study well to a fixed routine;
  • the role is in the domain you want after qualifying;
  • the employer clearly agrees to study leave before the exam.

Think twice if you have both groups left and your last attempt was close, or if the role is known for long hours at exam time. Final comes first. Leaving a job after three months to study isn't a failure. A failed attempt costs you six months.

Five kinds of roles

  1. Accounts and controllership. Books, month-end close, GST and TDS compliance, and audit support. Good preparation for a controller or finance manager path.
  2. Audit associate. Statutory or internal audit, either at a firm or in a company's internal audit team. A natural fit if audit was your articleship strength.
  3. Tax associate. Direct or indirect tax compliance and advisory, usually at a firm.
  4. FP&A or finance analyst. Budgets, MIS and variance analysis. These roles are competitive, and valuable if you want to move into corporate finance.
  5. Global capability centres and shared services. Record-to-report work, reporting under Indian and foreign GAAP, and reconciliations for a multinational's global operations. Some teams work to overseas time zones, so ask about shift timings.

Pick the role closest to what you want to do after you qualify. A year in the right domain is worth more than a slightly higher salary in the wrong one.

Where to find openings

  • Semi-qualified CA jobs on MSC, which you can filter by city and role.
  • Your articleship network: your principal, your managers and the clients you worked with. Tell them you're available. Plenty of semi-qualified roles are filled this way before they're ever posted.
  • Careers pages of Big 4 networks, mid-size firms and GCCs, many of which hire semi-qualified candidates.
  • LinkedIn, with a clear headline such as "Semi-qualified CA | Articleship in statutory audit | Looking for FP&A roles".
  • Seniors who took semi-qualified roles last year.

Salary: how to find a fair range

Pay varies a lot by city, company and role, so don't anchor on a number from a forwarded message.

  • Open ten real listings for your city and role, and note the range.
  • Ask two seniors what they were offered.

That gives you a better guide than any "average". When you're asked for your expectation, give a range based on this research, and say you're flexible for the right role.

The study-leave conversation

Raise study leave before you accept, not two months before the exam. A simple way to ask:

"I'm appearing for CA Final in <month year>. I'd need <number> weeks of study leave before the exam. Is that something the team can support?"

Employers who hire semi-qualified candidates know this question is coming. If the answer is vague, ask what the last semi-qualified hire did. If the answer is a clear no, you've learned something important before joining, not after.

Your CV and your interview

Put your status at the top of your CV, in plain words:

  • CA Final: groups cleared (with month and year), groups pending, and your planned attempt
  • CA Intermediate: cleared, with month and year
  • Articleship: completed, with your firm and your main areas

"CA (pursuing)" on its own hides the one thing a recruiter wants to know. Below your status, add three or four lines on work you did in articleship, with numbers: entities audited, returns filed, reconciliations handled. Our CV guide shows how.

Expect these questions:

  • "When is your Final, and how will you manage it alongside work?" Have a specific answer: which hours you'll study, and when your leave starts.
  • "Will you leave once you qualify?" Be honest. Say what would make you stay: the role, growth, the team.
  • "Walk me through your articleship." Specifics win here.

Plan for both outcomes

Before your result comes out, decide what you'll do either way:

  • If you clear, you're a newly qualified CA. You can sit ICAI campus placement, move up within your company, or search off campus. Our campus placement guide covers the first.
  • If you don't, keep the job, plan the next attempt six months later, and sort out your study leave early.

Either way, you'll be better placed than before, with work experience that many other freshers don't have.
